What Makes a Chinatown?

Over centuries of migration, Chinese communities have established enclaves known as Chinatowns in cities around the world, on nearly every continent. The history of Chinatowns began in the Tang dynasty in the 10th century, which is reflected in the Chinese names for Chinatowns 唐人街 - the streets of the Tang people.

This map traces all the Chinatowns around the world listed on Wikipedia, tracing these patterns of migrations across the world and the spatial and physical presence that Chinese migrants have left in the cities that they've settled in. But what makes these Chinatowns, Chinatowns? While all of the Chinatowns on these maps are defined by the presence of a substantial presence of Chinese commerce and businesses - and frequently marketed by the cities they belong to as must-see tourist destinations - only some remain (or ever were) residential hubs for Chinese communities.
